# CueLiez Demo Q&A (Tough Questions)

Use clear, calm, compliant answers. If a question asks for something CueLiez must not do, decline that claim and restate what it does.

## “Is this a lie detector?”
**Answer:** “No. CueLiez is not a lie detector, polygraph, or truth machine. It observes signals and testimony patterns and presents **cues** for a professional to review. Humans make the judgments.”

## “Can it tell if someone is lying?”
**Answer:** “No. It does not determine whether someone is lying or telling the truth. It can surface indicators — like possible inconsistencies, vagueness, or response shifts — so the professional can ask better follow-up questions.”

## “How accurate is it?”
**Answer:** “Its outputs are probabilistic observations, not findings. Accuracy depends on context, data quality, modality availability, and human interpretation. That’s why cues include confidence/evidence, are reviewed by professionals, and are never treated as automated verdicts. In evaluation, we’d measure usefulness for clarification/documentation workflows under appropriate governance — not ‘lie detection accuracy’.”

## “Can we use it to decide arrests, charges, clearance, or employment?”
**Answer:** “No. CueLiez must not be used for automated detention, charging, clearance, employment, immigration, custody, or similar decisions. It’s designed for human-led interview support and auditable observed indicators.”

## “Can clinicians/therapists use it to diagnose or judge credibility?”
**Answer:** “No. It’s not a medical device and not for diagnosis, treatment decisions, risk adjudication, or credibility/truthfulness determinations. In clinical/supportive contexts it can only be used, if at all, as communication/process support under proper consent, policy, and professional judgment.”

## “What about bias/fairness?”
**Answer:** “That’s an important concern. CueLiez treats outputs as cues for human review, not decisions, and it’s designed to support fairer inquiry through clearer questions and documentation. Any deployment should include domain-appropriate governance, consent, training, evaluation on representative contexts, and human accountability. We should never treat a cue as proof about a person.”

## “Do you record people? Is it covert?”
**Answer:** “Use must follow applicable law, policy, and consent requirements. High-stakes modes enforce consent gates in the system workflow; operators are responsible for required notices and lawful authority. CueLiez is not designed for covert or warrantless misuse outside lawful authority/policy.”

## “What does the audio cue do?”
**Answer:** “Audio cues are optional attention prompts for the professional — for example, a priority tone and a short script indicating there’s an observation to review. They don’t announce guilt, deception, or conclusions.”

## “What gets stored?”
**Answer:** “When sealed through the hardened path, the system stores observed indicators with confidence, method/model context, disclaimers, and firewall metadata — marked as `observed_indicator`, grounded, and not a definitive finding. It rejects verdict-style labels and forbidden annotations.”

## “Why ‘LIE’ in the name?”
**Answer:** “In CueLiez, LIE stands for **Legal Interview Engine**. The product does not detect lies; the name expansion and language rules make that explicit.”

## “Can I see the raw model decide?”
**Answer:** “You can see the observation output: label/category, evidence, confidence, method/model identity, and the suggested cue. But the key design choice is that these are observations for human review — not a final decision engine.”
